The question

For each of the 20 amino acids, which strains carry a breakdown (utilization) pathway that frees its nitrogen, judged only by the pathway's breakdown-only enzymes? How does that differ between clades and from marine Synechococcus? A standalone, descriptive map: no hypothesis, no formal statistics. Transporters (uptake) are set aside for now.

How a pathway is scored

amino acid→ breakdown-only step→ shared step (also builds amino acids)→ breakdown-only step→ N freed: NH₄⁺ · glutamate · urea

Only the breakdown-only steps (orange border) count. The step that frees the nitrogen must be present for "complete", even if it is shared. Shared steps are shown greyed out as context, because every strain needs them to build amino acids, so they say nothing about utilization.

complete: all breakdown-only steps found partial: some found (k of n) absent: none found can't be judged: the pathway has only shared steps

Pathways and steps come from a catalogue you approve (KEGG IDs, checked against KEGG). Genes are matched by enzyme ID, never by gene name. Broad and D-amino-acid enzymes go in their own column.

Decisions locked at approval

Glutamate dehydrogenase = shared

It also captures ammonium, so it is not evidence of breakdown. Glutamate will likely be "can't be judged".

Urease = shared

It serves urea use, so it cannot make arginine "complete".

One rule for "shared"

Shared = also needed to build something (amino acids, polyamines, cofactors), for central metabolism, or for another N source.

What the MED4 pilot showed (draft catalogue)

Asparagine: complete

Asparaginase (PMM0821, ansA) frees NH₄⁺. One breakdown-only step, found.

Arginine: absent

No arginase or arginine deiminase. The agmatine route is complete but all-shared (speA, speB, urease), so it is flagged, not counted.

Histidine, lysine: absent

No hut enzymes for histidine. Lysine decarboxylase (cadA) is found, but it makes a polyamine, so it counts as shared.

Other MED4 hits are shared biosynthesis enzymes (ilvE, hisC, ilvA, glycine cleavage), which are not counted. One threonine hit (PMM1468) is flagged: its product is a biotin enzyme. Full pilot figure: spike/reports/med4_pilot_summary.html.

How we will know the method works

Must be found

  • glnA (glutamine synthetase) in all 45 strains: tests the gene-matching
  • 2–3 aminotransferases that amino-acid building needs (e.g. ilvE, hisC), in every complete genome

Must come out empty

  • One enzyme absent from cyanobacteria in general, named in advance. A hit is inspected as a possible annotation error.

Findings, not failures

  • All strains look the same
  • Most amino acids: no utilization pathway
  • Complete pathways only in some strains (e.g. larger low-light genomes)

Caveats that travel with every result

  • Pathway ≠ using outside amino acids. These enzymes also act on amino acids made inside the cell. With uptake set aside, we cannot say a strain uses external amino acids as an N source.
  • Annotation ≠ function. An enzyme-family match does not prove activity or substrate.
  • "Absent" may mean "not sequenced". NCBI assembly status is used to flag incomplete genomes.
  • No heterotroph check. "Absent" is reported as "not found with these annotations".
  • Small clades (HLIII, HLIV, LLIII) cannot stand for their clade.
  • Early look: MED4 results were seen before the rules were locked. This is recorded in the plan notebook.
