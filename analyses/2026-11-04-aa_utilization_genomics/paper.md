# Amino-acid utilization pathways across Prochlorococcus genomes

## Question

For each of the 20 standard amino acids, which *Prochlorococcus* strains encode a breakdown pathway that frees the amino acid's nitrogen, judged by the pathway's enzymes that serve only in breakdown? How does this differ among the high-light and low-light clades, and compared with marine *Synechococcus*?

## Background

*Prochlorococcus* lives in oligotrophic ocean waters, where dissolved inorganic nitrogen is scarce and dissolved organic nitrogen, including free amino acids, is a potential alternative source. To use an amino acid as a nitrogen source, a cell must remove its amino nitrogen. It can do so directly, releasing ammonium through a deaminase, ammonia-lyase, dehydrogenase, oxidase or amidase. Or it can transfer the amino group to 2-oxoglutarate through an aminotransferase, forming glutamate. Several of these enzymes are also needed for amino-acid biosynthesis. Here we survey 45 sequenced *Prochlorococcus* genomes spanning the high-light clades HLI–HLIV and the low-light clades LLI–LLIV, with *Synechococcus* genomes for comparison. Because several of these enzymes also serve biosynthesis or central metabolism, their presence says little about breakdown. For each genome we therefore score each amino acid's breakdown pathways by the enzymes that serve only in breakdown, requiring the step that frees the nitrogen to be present.
