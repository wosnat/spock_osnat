# High-light-inducible (hli) genes in *Prochlorococcus*: light response or broad stress response?

## Question

*Prochlorococcus* MED4 carries 23 genes encoding small high-light-inducible proteins (HLIPs). We ask whether their transcripts respond to light, and whether they respond across stresses other than light. Where the available experiments allow it, we also ask whether that response is independent of light. A same-method survey of other cyanobacterial strains provides a broader view across stresses. The analysis is exploratory: it reuses published transcriptome experiments, several of which were inspected while the analysis was planned.

## Background

HLIPs are single-helix members of the chlorophyll a/b-binding protein superfamily. They are thought to protect the photosynthetic apparatus when absorbed light exceeds what photosynthesis can use. The *hli* gene family is greatly expanded in high-light-adapted *Prochlorococcus*; MED4 has 23 copies, many of them in clusters and near-identical in sequence. Several *Prochlorococcus* transcriptome studies report *hli* induction. Because a cell under nutrient or CO2 limitation uses less of the light it absorbs, induction under such stresses could reflect either a light-driven photoprotective response or a broader stress response. The experiments brought together here comprise 29 MED4 transcriptome comparisons from 13 studies, covering light onset and photoperiod, photosynthetic electron transport, nitrogen, phosphorus, iron, CO2, salt and phage infection, together with non-stress comparisons.
